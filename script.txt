Today I’m comparing no-code and low-code.
Why teams use them: they deliver simple apps and workflows fast with visual builders, less manual testing, and managed hosting. That gives faster time-to-market, lighter maintenance, and automatic updates—great for internal tools, forms, dashboards, and process automation. They also increase business agility by letting product and operations iterate with IT rather than waiting in a queue.

But speed has a price.
There’s vendor lock-in—you often don’t own the source code.
There are feature limits—unique logic, complex integrations, or high performance can exceed the platform.
And there’s governance risk—ungoverned ‘citizen apps’ can create security or compliance gaps.

My rule of thumb:

Choose no/low-code for MVPs, prototypes, pilots, and internal systems.

Choose traditional code for core, highly customized, or performance-critical products.
Either way, set guardrails: require data export and open APIs, define SLAs, run performance/security tests, and keep an exit plan from day one.
